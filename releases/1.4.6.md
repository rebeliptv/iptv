## What's New

#### New

- **Watch up to four channels or games at once.** A new Multiview tab puts up to four pictures on one screen — any mix of channels and live games — with sound from whichever one you click. Each picture uses one of your simultaneous streams, so the page shows how many you have left for your other devices; leaving the page frees them again.
- **Hockey games get a page built for hockey.** The two starting goalies side by side with their save percentages, team stats split into goaltending and skating, season leaders, and the period-by-period score once the game is under way.

#### Live sport

- **A game only shows as watchable once a stream is really running.** Games used to look ready hours early, or claim a feed had been found when nothing behind them worked — and then play nothing when you pressed the button. A game now says it's watchable only once real video is coming through, and says so plainly when it isn't.
- **A game that loses its stream moves to a working one.** There are more streams behind each game than before, they're no longer all the same stream underneath, and your server now starts on the best one available rather than whichever it happens to open first. When one stops, it moves to a genuinely different one instead of failing the same way twice.
- **Tonight's games are listed from the morning.** Games starting later in the day, and fight cards and races a few days out, used to be missed entirely depending on when your server last refreshed. They're all picked up now. College football also has more streams behind it.

#### Playback

- **Channels show a picture the moment they open.** Tuning in could leave you on a black frame for a couple of seconds after the stream had already started arriving. Channels now start on a complete picture — and open a little quicker than before, not slower.
- **Live games build their buffer before they start.** Tuning into a game started almost immediately on a second or two of cushion, however high you'd set "Seconds to buffer" — which is backwards, because the opening moments are when a game's stream is least steady. It now banks what you asked for first, so an unsteady stream is smoothed over instead of stuttering.
- **Channels start at live, and stay where you are.** Some channels began playback from as much as two hours earlier. Others jumped backwards and replayed the last half-minute when a stream renewed or switched. Both are fixed.
- **A channel stops hopping between providers while it's playing fine.** Some providers renew their stream every half-minute — normal, and invisible to you — and your server was reading that as a failing feed and moving the channel every couple of minutes, freezing the picture and resuming at a different point each time. A genuinely bad feed is still dropped, slightly faster than before.
- **Channels from the Rebel relay are more reliable.** They no longer give up while the relay is still finding a working source, the picture no longer breaks up when the relay switches source behind a channel, and a channel whose access quietly expired now recovers on its own instead of needing a restart.

#### Your setup

- **ABC, CBS, NBC and FOX always play your local station.** These four carry your own market's station, but when it hiccuped they could open on — or switch mid-show to — another city's, with your local guide still on screen. They now play your market's station and nothing else; when it's off the air the channel says so, and comes back when the station does.
- **The player's controls stay reachable, so you can go fullscreen.** The controls were being taken away from the video a couple of seconds after you stopped moving the mouse — not faded out, removed — taking fullscreen and volume with them. They now stay with the video while it plays, so a click always lands. The cursor still gets out of the way when you're not interacting.
- **Your own custom sources no longer count against your simultaneous-streams limit.** That limit is there to stay within what the channel sources allow, but sources you added yourself are fetched inside your own network and never touch them — so they were using up an allowance that was never protecting anything.

## Update

Already running Rebel IPTV? Update to this version — your data (the `iptv-data` volume) is kept. From the dashboard, click **Update now** on the Dashboard or in **Settings → Server** (on the `:latest` image with the Docker socket mounted). Or from the command line:

```bash
docker pull rebeliptv/iptv:1.4.6
docker compose up -d
```

Setting up a **new** instance? The step-by-step guide at **https://www.rebeliptv.com** builds a Docker configuration for your system — ports, timezone, in-app updates, and more — so you copy a setup that fits your box instead of a generic one.

## Endpoints

| URL | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| `http://<server>:8080/playlist?key=YOUR_KEY` | M3U playlist |
| `http://<server>:8080/epg?key=YOUR_KEY` | EPG guide (XML) |
| `http://<server>:8080/epg.gz?key=YOUR_KEY` | EPG guide (gzip) |
| `http://<server>:8080` | Web dashboard |

> **Note:** The `?key=` parameter is only required if an API key has been generated in the web dashboard under **Settings**.
