## What's New

- **The pinned ABC, CBS, NBC and FOX channels now use YOUR local stations, not always New York's.** By default they pick a market from your server's timezone (Eastern → New York, Central → Chicago, Mountain → Denver, Pacific → Los Angeles); or set your exact **home market** in **Settings → Content → Local Market** — Boston, Seattle, Dallas, Atlanta and ~35 others — and those four channels switch to that city's local affiliates for **both the live feed and the guide**. If your local station goes down they borrow the biggest station in your own timezone instead (Boston → New York, Seattle → Los Angeles), listings included, so the picture and the guide always agree; they never borrow across a timezone. CW, PBS and ION have no local option and stay on their national feed.
- **First-run setup now has a "Your content" step.** A new instance can pick its local market, turn live sports events on or off, and choose which channel countries to carry right in the setup wizard, instead of finding those settings afterward.
- **A redesigned dashboard, with a new Delivery Health panel.** The dashboard uses more of a large screen instead of a narrow centre column, and the numbers you actually check — how many people are watching, how much data you've served — are in the row of tiles at the top rather than buried in a list. The new **Delivery Health** panel shows how reliably your box has been serving: how often a channel switched to a backup feed and why, whether any channel ran out of working feeds while someone was watching, and how many feeds are currently resting — hover any label for an explanation of what it means. Your last refresh now shows how long it took, and the **Channels** page shows 36 channels at a time by default, remembers the page size you pick, and lists channels in the same order as your playlist and guide.
- **Game pages are now built for the sport you're watching.** **Baseball** gets a two-column layout: one side compares the two teams — win probability, records and the batting / pitching / fielding numbers, each as a bar in the teams' colours rather than two figures marooned at opposite edges of the screen — and the other shows the pitching matchup, season leaders, recent form and the injury report. **Soccer** (MLS, NWSL and internationals) gets recent form across the top, then head-to-head history beside the top scorers, plus a running timeline of goals, cards and substitutions once the match kicks off. And **any live game** now opens on a broadcast-style scoreboard — each team on the outside, the score either side of a period-by-period box score, the clock beneath — with the league, status and matchup in the same place for every sport. Other sports keep the standard layout for now.
- **Live streams hold up better.** Four improvements to how playback behaves when a feed misbehaves. Feeds that arrive in **bursts** — several seconds of video at once, then quiet — are now smoothed out as your box receives them, instead of that silence stalling the player and forcing a reconnect. Channels and events **start playing sooner** after you select them, without giving up that smoothing. **Failover is steadier in three ways**: a feed that hiccups briefly is no longer switched away from just because it took a moment to catch back up; a channel whose other feeds were resting after an earlier failure no longer clings to a stuttering one indefinitely; and when it does switch, it avoids backup feeds in a video format your player can't follow — the case that made Jellyfin and Plex stop playback outright rather than just glitch. And in the browser, a channel that **freezes on a still frame** with the connection still live now spots that and reconnects itself.
- **Your sports channels now appear when there's actually something to watch.** An event's channel used to be added to your playlist as soon as a feed *page* was found for it — usually hours before the game, and sometimes for a game whose feed never turned up at all — so your player's lineup filled with sports channels that spun or errored if you tuned to them. An event is now added once its feed is confirmed live, within about a minute of it starting, and only if it does. At the other end, a finished game no longer vanishes from the playlist the instant it goes final: it stays for as long as its feed keeps running, so the channel rides through post-game coverage instead of dropping mid-broadcast.
- **Other fixes.** Channels like Nickelodeon, SYFY, the Disney channels, Animal Planet and Travel Channel could pick up a **West-coast feed about three hours behind the guide** — those feeds are now recognised and skipped in favour of the national one. Installs updating from an older version to the country-identified-channels release could hit a **one-time database-upgrade error and fail to boot**; the upgrade now fills in the columns it was missing instead of aborting. And **"Active now"** no longer gets stuck — the viewer count could stay high after everyone stopped watching, or drop to zero if you cleared your metrics mid-stream.

## Update

Already running Rebel IPTV? Update to this version — your data (the `iptv-data` volume) is kept. From the dashboard, click **Update now** on the Dashboard or in **Settings → Server** (on the `:latest` image with the Docker socket mounted). Or from the command line:

```bash
docker pull rebeliptv/iptv:1.3.6
docker compose up -d
```

Setting up a **new** instance? The step-by-step guide at **https://www.rebeliptv.com** builds a Docker configuration for your system — ports, timezone, in-app updates, and more — so you copy a setup that fits your box instead of a generic one.

## Endpoints

| URL | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| `http://<server>:8080/playlist?key=YOUR_KEY` | M3U playlist |
| `http://<server>:8080/epg?key=YOUR_KEY` | EPG guide (XML) |
| `http://<server>:8080/epg.gz?key=YOUR_KEY` | EPG guide (gzip) |
| `http://<server>:8080` | Web dashboard |

> **Note:** The `?key=` parameter is only required if an API key has been generated in the web dashboard under **Settings**.
