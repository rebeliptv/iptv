## What's New

Use your own guide for any channel, see which game is coming up on your sports channels, and start channels sooner and more reliably in Plex, Emby and Jellyfin.

#### Your guide

- **Use your own guide for any channel.** Settings → Sources → Your own guide takes an XMLTV guide of your own, at a URL or as a file in your custom-epg folder. Its listings replace your server's for every channel it lists, matched by the id your server's playlist gives each channel (its tvg-id, such as us-espn), and your server's guide fills any time it leaves empty. If it fails to load, the last good copy stays in use and the panel says why, along with any channel ids in it that match nothing and any listings it had to leave out because their times were wrong.
- **The guide says which game is coming up on a sports channel.** The time before each game's pregame was empty in the guide. It now lists "Upcoming:" and the game, on league channels, team channels and a game's own channel alike, from the start of the game's day or the end of the game before it.

#### Tuning in

- **Channels already playing start sooner in Plex, Emby and Jellyfin.** When you tuned a channel your server was already playing, or one already playing on the Rebel relay, your app got only 3 seconds of your server's buffer at once and the rest at the speed it plays: Plex showed the first picture and held it for about 10 seconds while it buffered. Your server now hands your app the buffer at once. With stream stats on, the buffer your app holds shows as the player's share. Jellyfin also read the stream at the speed it plays, so the media server link now turns off "Read input at native frame rate" on the tuner it keeps, and the setup steps under Settings → Players recommend it off: on such a channel Jellyfin then had 12 seconds ready as soon as you tuned, and Emby 16 seconds in under 4.
- **Channels start sooner in Plex the first time you tune them after your server starts.** On a server that uses the Rebel relay, the first tune of each channel sent your player nothing until the channel's whole 15-second buffer had arrived. Your server now sends the Connecting screen as soon as the channel's stream starts to arrive, usually within a couple of seconds, so your player can start getting ready while the channel loads.
- **Channels you've watched start their Connecting screen at once after your server restarts.** The first tune of each channel after an update or restart used to wait for the channel's stream to arrive before showing the Connecting screen, 10 seconds or more on a slow one. Your server now remembers what each channel you've watched in the past week sounds like, so the screen comes up straight away.
- **A channel's sound starts with its picture after the Connecting screen.** On a first tune in Plex, Emby or Jellyfin, a channel's picture could play for a second or two before its sound came in. It now starts with the picture.

#### Channels and playback

- **A channel whose first stream is broken moves on to the next one sooner.** When the first stream your server tried for a channel stopped sending after its first moments, or brought its picture with no sound, your server could hold the channel for more than 20 seconds before trying another, or play it silent, and Plex gives up on a channel about 25 seconds in. Your server now moves on after 10 seconds of nothing, or 8 seconds of picture without sound, when the channel has another stream to try.
- **A channel that keeps failing to play is tried less often.** While your player stayed on a channel that couldn't play, your server tried it again every 15 seconds for as long as you left it there. It now waits longer each time, up to 5 minutes, and still moves the moment the channel's listing changes.
- **A source that refuses a channel is set aside sooner.** On a server that finds its own streams, a source that refused to play a channel kept the channel listed, and every tune tried it first. Your server now sets that source aside for a while, as it does one that fails to open.
- **Games whose stream jumps ahead don't lose those seconds any more.** Some game streams list only their last few seconds and every so often jump ahead faster than your server can fetch them. The seconds they jumped over were lost, each time taking that much out of your buffer, until the Reconnecting screen came up. Those seconds are now fetched anyway, since the stream still has them for a while after it moves on, whether your server finds its own streams or uses the Rebel relay.
- **UK and Canadian channels get back streams they'd lost.** Since late October, Sky Sports, TNT Sports, TSN and other UK and Canadian channels had lost some of their streams, after a place your server finds them began naming its channels differently. Your server now reads the new names.

#### Your server

- **A server on the Rebel relay rides out a dropped connection.** When the connection to the relay dropped partway through a refresh, your games and guide waited for the next refresh, an hour later. Your server now asks the relay again.
- **Purge and rescrape says what it keeps.** Its question listed your channel map and numbering among what it deletes, yet your lineup stays: the channels in it, their order and numbers, and your own groups, along with your settings, custom playlists and your own guide. It now lists only what it deletes (the channels' streams, the guide, the games and saved show images) and says what stays.

## Update

Already running Rebel IPTV? Update to this version — your data (the `iptv-data` volume) is kept. From the dashboard, click **Update now** on the Dashboard or in **Settings → System** (on the `:latest` image with the Docker socket mounted). Or from the command line:

```bash
docker pull rebeliptv/iptv:latest
docker compose up -d
```

On an image pinned to a version, change its tag in your compose file to `:1.5.2` and run `docker compose up -d`.

Setting up a **new** instance? The step-by-step guide at **https://www.rebeliptv.com** builds a Docker configuration for your system — ports, timezone, in-app updates, and more — so you copy a setup that fits your box instead of a generic one.

Image: `rebeliptv/iptv:1.5.2` (also published as `rebeliptv/iptv:latest`).

## Endpoints

| URL | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| `http://<server>:8080/playlist?key=YOUR_KEY` | M3U playlist |
| `http://<server>:8080/epg?key=YOUR_KEY` | EPG guide (XML) |
| `http://<server>:8080/epg.gz?key=YOUR_KEY` | EPG guide (gzip) |
| `http://<server>:8080` | Web dashboard |

> **Note:** The `?key=` parameter is only required if an API key has been generated in the web dashboard under **Settings → Security**.
